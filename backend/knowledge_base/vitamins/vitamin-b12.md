## What it measures
Vitamin B12 (also called cobalamin) is needed to make healthy red blood cells, to keep nerves working properly, and to make DNA. The body cannot make B12 itself, so it must come from food. It is found mainly in animal foods such as meat, fish, eggs and dairy, and in some fortified foods. The test measures the amount of vitamin B12 in your blood. It is often done together with a test for **folate**, another B vitamin that works closely with B12.

## Why the test is done
Doctors check B12 when a blood count shows large red blood cells (a high MCV) or anaemia, or when someone has symptoms such as tiredness, pins and needles, numbness, memory problems or a sore tongue. It is also checked in people at higher risk of low levels, such as people who eat little or no animal food, older adults, people with stomach or bowel conditions, and people taking certain long-term medicines.

## Understanding the reference range
Results are usually given in pg/mL or pmol/L. A common adult range is about 200 to 900 pg/mL, but labs differ, and there is a "grey zone" near the lower limit where results are hard to interpret. In that case doctors may order extra tests. A normal B12 level in the blood does not always rule out a problem, so doctors also consider symptoms and other results.

## What high values can be associated with
A high B12 level is often associated with taking supplements or B12 injections. It can sometimes be associated with liver conditions or other conditions, which your doctor may look into if there is no obvious reason.

## What low values can be associated with
A low B12 level can be associated with a diet low in animal foods, problems absorbing B12 from the gut (for example a condition called pernicious anaemia, or after some stomach surgery), older age, and some long-term medicines. Low B12 can lead to anaemia with large red cells and to nerve symptoms. Only your doctor can interpret your result and decide on next steps.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Is my B12 level low, borderline or normal?
- Could my diet or any medicine be affecting my B12?
- Should my folate level be checked as well?
