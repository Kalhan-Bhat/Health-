## What it measures
Vitamin D helps your body absorb calcium, which keeps bones and teeth strong. It also plays a part in muscle function and the immune system. Your skin makes vitamin D when it is exposed to sunlight, and small amounts come from foods such as oily fish, eggs and fortified foods. The usual blood test measures **25-hydroxy vitamin D**, the form that best shows how much vitamin D your body has stored.

## Why the test is done
Doctors may check vitamin D if someone has bone pain, weak bones (osteoporosis) or muscle weakness, or if they are at higher risk of low levels. Higher-risk groups include people who get little sun, older adults, people with darker skin, people with conditions that affect how food is absorbed, and people taking certain medicines. The test is also used to monitor treatment.

## Understanding the reference range
Results are given in ng/mL or nmol/L (1 ng/mL is about 2.5 nmol/L). Labs and expert groups use slightly different cut-offs. A common guide is: below 20 ng/mL is considered deficient, 20 to 29 ng/mL insufficient, and 30 to 100 ng/mL sufficient. Some groups use lower cut-offs. Vitamin D levels can change with the seasons, and are often lower after winter.

## What high values can be associated with
High vitamin D levels are uncommon. They are usually associated with taking large amounts of vitamin D supplements over a long time. Very high levels can raise blood calcium. Your doctor can tell whether your level is a concern.

## What low values can be associated with
Low vitamin D is very common. It can be associated with little sun exposure, darker skin, older age, excess weight, a diet low in vitamin D, conditions that affect absorption, kidney or liver conditions, and some medicines. Over time, low levels can be linked with weaker bones. Only your doctor can tell what your result means for you and whether any action is needed.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Is my vitamin D level low enough to need any action?
- What might be causing my level to be low?
- When should I have my vitamin D checked again?
