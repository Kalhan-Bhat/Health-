## What it measures
Iron is a mineral your body needs to make haemoglobin, the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. Iron studies are a group of blood tests that show how much iron you have and how it is being used and stored:
- **Ferritin:** a protein that stores iron. The amount in the blood reflects how much iron is stored in the body.
- **Serum iron:** the amount of iron moving in the blood right now.
- **TIBC (total iron binding capacity)** and **transferrin:** show how much iron the blood's carrier protein could carry.
- **Transferrin saturation:** the share of that carrier protein that is actually carrying iron.

## Why the test is done
Doctors often order iron studies when a blood count shows low haemoglobin, or small and pale red cells (a low MCV and MCH). They help show whether low iron is the reason. The tests are also used to check for too much iron in the body, and to monitor treatment.

## Understanding the reference range
Ranges differ between labs and between men and women. Ferritin ranges are often around 20 to 250 ng/mL for adult men and 10 to 120 ng/mL for adult women. Low ferritin is one of the clearest signs of low iron stores. However, ferritin also rises during infection or inflammation, so a normal ferritin does not always rule out low iron. Serum iron changes during the day and after meals, so labs may ask for a morning or fasting sample.

## What high values can be associated with
High ferritin can be associated with inflammation, infection, liver conditions, regular alcohol use, and conditions in which the body stores too much iron, such as haemochromatosis. High transferrin saturation can also point towards iron overload. Only your doctor can tell what applies to you.

## What low values can be associated with
Low ferritin and low transferrin saturation are commonly associated with low iron stores. Low iron can have many causes, such as heavy periods, pregnancy, a diet low in iron, blood loss from the gut, and problems absorbing iron. Finding the reason matters, so your doctor may suggest further tests.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Do my iron studies show that my iron stores are low?
- What could be causing low iron in my case?
- Could an infection or inflammation be affecting my ferritin result?
