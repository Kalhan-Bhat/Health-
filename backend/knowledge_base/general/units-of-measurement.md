## Why units matter
Every number on a lab report comes with a unit. The unit tells you what was counted or weighed and in how much blood. The same result can look very different in two units. For example, a blood sugar of 100 mg/dL is the same as about 5.6 mmol/L. Always read the number and the unit together, and compare them with the range on the same report.

## Common units and what they mean
- **g/dL (grams per decilitre):** the weight of a substance in one tenth of a litre of blood. Used for haemoglobin and albumin.
- **mg/dL (milligrams per decilitre):** a smaller weight in the same amount of blood. Common for cholesterol, glucose and creatinine in countries such as India and the United States.
- **mmol/L (millimoles per litre):** counts the number of molecules rather than their weight. Used for glucose and cholesterol in the UK and many other countries, and for sodium and potassium almost everywhere.
- **cells/µL or /cumm (cells per microlitre):** the number of cells in a tiny drop of blood. Used for white blood cells and platelets. Some labs write the same count as "x10³/µL" or "x10⁹/L".
- **%:** a percentage, such as the share of blood made up of red cells (hematocrit) or the share of each type of white cell.
- **fL (femtolitres) and pg (picograms):** very small units of size and weight, used for red blood cell measurements such as MCV and MCH.
- **IU/L, µIU/mL, ng/mL, pg/mL:** units used for enzymes, hormones and vitamins.

## Different ways of writing big numbers
Platelet and white cell counts are large numbers. A lab may write 2,45,000 (Indian grouping), 245,000, or 245 x10³/µL. All of these describe the same count.

## Understanding the reference range
The reference range is always written in the same unit as the result. If you compare results from two labs, check that they use the same unit first. Your doctor or the lab can help convert between units.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- My earlier report used different units. How do the two results compare?
- Which unit should I use if I keep my own record of results?
