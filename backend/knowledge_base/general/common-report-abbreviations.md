## Why reports use abbreviations
Lab reports have little space, so tests are often written as short letter codes. Once you know the common ones, a report becomes much easier to follow.

## Blood count abbreviations
- **CBC:** complete blood count, a group of tests on blood cells.
- **Hb or Hgb:** haemoglobin, the oxygen-carrying protein in red cells.
- **HCT or PCV:** hematocrit or packed cell volume, the share of blood made up of red cells.
- **RBC:** red blood cell count.
- **WBC or TLC:** white blood cell count, also called total leucocyte count.
- **DLC:** differential leucocyte count, the mix of white cell types.
- **PLT:** platelet count.
- **MCV, MCH, MCHC:** measurements of red cell size and haemoglobin content.
- **RDW:** red cell distribution width, how much red cells vary in size.

## Chemistry abbreviations
- **FBS or FBG:** fasting blood sugar or glucose. **PPBS:** blood sugar after a meal.
- **HbA1c:** average blood sugar over about three months.
- **LDL, HDL, VLDL, TG:** types of cholesterol and triglycerides.
- **BUN:** blood urea nitrogen. **eGFR:** estimated glomerular filtration rate (kidney filtering).
- **LFT:** liver function tests. **ALT, AST, ALP:** liver enzymes.
- **KFT or RFT:** kidney or renal function tests.
- **TSH, T3, T4:** thyroid tests.
- **Na, K, Cl:** sodium, potassium and chloride.

## Flag abbreviations
- **H or ↑:** above the reference range. **L or ↓:** below it.
- **HH or LL:** far above or below the range, often treated as critical.
- **WNL:** within normal limits. **N/A or QNS:** not available or quantity not sufficient.

## Understanding the reference range
Abbreviations do not change how a result is read. Each result is still compared with the range printed next to it.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What does this abbreviation on my report stand for?
- Which of these tests matter most in my situation?
