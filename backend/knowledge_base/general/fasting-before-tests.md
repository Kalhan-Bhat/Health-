## What fasting means
Fasting before a blood test means not eating or drinking anything except plain water for a set time before the sample is taken. The usual time is 8 to 12 hours, so many people book a morning appointment and fast overnight. The lab or doctor will say whether fasting is needed and for how long.

## Why some tests need fasting
Food and drinks are digested into sugar and fats that enter the blood. For a few hours after a meal, these levels rise. Tests that measure them can give a higher result simply because you ate recently. Fasting gives a steady starting point, so results can be compared fairly with the reference range and with earlier tests.

## Tests that often need fasting
- **Fasting blood glucose:** measures sugar after an overnight fast.
- **Lipid profile:** triglycerides in particular rise after meals. Some labs now accept non-fasting cholesterol tests, so follow the lab's instructions.
- **Some other tests**, such as certain iron or vitamin tests, may have their own preparation rules.

Many common tests, such as a complete blood count, thyroid tests and HbA1c, usually do not need fasting.

## Practical tips
- Drinking plain water is usually allowed and helps make the blood draw easier.
- Avoid coffee, tea, juice, chewing gum and smoking during the fast unless told otherwise.
- Do not stop any prescribed medicine to fast without asking your doctor first.
- If you have diabetes, ask your doctor how to fast safely.
- Tell the person taking the sample if you did not manage to fast.

## Understanding the reference range
Fasting and non-fasting results can have different ranges. If you ate before a test that needed fasting, a value may look higher than it really is. Your doctor may suggest repeating it.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Do I need to fast for this test, and for how many hours?
- Can I take my usual medicines before the test?
- I forgot to fast. Should the test be repeated?
