## What it measures
**Creatinine** is a waste product made when muscles use energy. Healthy kidneys filter creatinine out of the blood and remove it in urine. If the kidneys are not filtering well, creatinine can build up in the blood. The test measures the amount of creatinine in your blood, usually in mg/dL.

**eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate)** is calculated from your creatinine, age and sometimes sex. It estimates how much blood your kidneys filter each minute, and is reported in mL/min/1.73m². Higher eGFR means better filtering.

## Why the test is done
Creatinine and eGFR are the main tests used to check kidney function. They are part of routine metabolic panels and kidney function tests (KFT or RFT). Doctors check them more often in people with diabetes, high blood pressure or heart disease, before and during some medicines, and when kidney problems are suspected.

## Understanding the reference range
Typical adult creatinine ranges are about 0.7 to 1.3 mg/dL for men and 0.6 to 1.1 mg/dL for women, but labs differ. Creatinine depends on muscle mass, so very muscular people may have higher values and people with little muscle may have lower values. An eGFR of 60 or more is usually considered in the normal range. A value below 60 for three months or more may suggest reduced kidney function. eGFR naturally falls slowly with age.

## What high values can be associated with
A creatinine above the range (or an eGFR below the range) can be associated with reduced kidney function, dehydration, a lot of muscle mass, eating a large amount of meat shortly before the test, intense exercise, and some medicines. Short-term changes can happen during illness. Only your doctor can tell what your result means for you.

## What low values can be associated with
A low creatinine is usually not a concern. It can be associated with low muscle mass, older age, pregnancy, or a low-meat diet. A high eGFR is generally not a concern either.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What do my creatinine and eGFR say about how well my kidneys are working?
- Could dehydration, exercise or a medicine have affected my result?
- Should these tests be repeated, or should I have a urine test as well?
