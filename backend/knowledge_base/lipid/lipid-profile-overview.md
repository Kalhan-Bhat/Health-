## What it measures
A lipid profile is a blood test that measures fats (lipids) in your blood. Cholesterol and triglycerides do not mix with blood on their own, so they travel in tiny packages called lipoproteins. A standard lipid profile usually reports:
- **Total cholesterol:** all the cholesterol in your blood.
- **LDL cholesterol:** often called "bad" cholesterol.
- **HDL cholesterol:** often called "good" cholesterol.
- **Triglycerides:** a type of fat that stores energy.
- Sometimes also **VLDL cholesterol**, **non-HDL cholesterol** and ratios such as total cholesterol to HDL.

## Why the test is done
Doctors use the lipid profile to estimate the risk of heart disease and stroke, because high levels of some lipids are linked with fatty build-up in the arteries. It is often part of routine check-ups for adults and may be repeated more often for people with diabetes, high blood pressure, a family history of heart disease, or who are on treatment for cholesterol.

## Understanding the reference range
Lipid results are usually compared with target values set by health guidelines, not with a range drawn from healthy people. That is why many reports show one-sided limits, such as "less than 200 mg/dL", or bands labelled "desirable", "borderline high" and "high". Your personal target may be different depending on your overall heart health risk. Some labs ask you to fast before the test, mainly because triglycerides rise after meals.

## What high values can be associated with
Higher total cholesterol, LDL or triglycerides can be associated with diet, low physical activity, excess weight, smoking, family history, diabetes, an underactive thyroid, kidney or liver conditions, and some medicines. They are linked with a higher long-term risk of heart and blood vessel disease. Your doctor will look at the whole picture.

## What low values can be associated with
A low HDL is linked with higher heart risk. Very low total cholesterol or LDL is uncommon and is not usually a concern, though doctors may look into it in some situations.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What do my cholesterol results mean for my overall heart health risk?
- What target values apply to me?
- Do I need to fast before my next lipid test, and when should it be repeated?
