## What it measures
**VLDL (very-low-density lipoprotein)** is made by the liver and mainly carries triglycerides to the body's tissues. As VLDL gives up its triglycerides, it slowly changes into LDL. Most labs do not measure VLDL directly. Instead, they estimate it from the triglyceride result, often as triglycerides divided by five (in mg/dL).

**Non-HDL cholesterol** is total cholesterol minus HDL cholesterol. It adds together all the types of cholesterol that can build up in arteries, including LDL and VLDL. Some reports also show the **total cholesterol to HDL ratio**.

## Why the test is done
These values give doctors a fuller picture of cholesterol-related heart risk. Non-HDL cholesterol is especially useful when triglycerides are high or when the person was not fasting, because it does not depend on the LDL calculation. VLDL helps show how much cholesterol is travelling with triglycerides.

## Understanding the reference range
Common guides are: VLDL less than 30 mg/dL, and non-HDL cholesterol less than 130 mg/dL. In mmol/L, 130 mg/dL is about 3.4. A total cholesterol to HDL ratio below about 5 is often considered better, and lower is better still. Labs and guidelines differ, and your own target depends on your overall heart risk.

## What high values can be associated with
A high VLDL usually goes along with high triglycerides. It can be associated with similar factors, such as diet, excess weight, low physical activity, alcohol, diabetes and genes. A high non-HDL cholesterol can be associated with the same factors that raise LDL. Both are linked with a higher long-term risk of heart and blood vessel disease. Your doctor can tell what your results mean for you.

## What low values can be associated with
Low VLDL and low non-HDL cholesterol are generally not a concern and are usually considered favourable for heart health.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What does my non-HDL cholesterol add to what my LDL already tells you?
- Is my VLDL raised because of my triglycerides?
- What target should I aim for, given my overall heart risk?
