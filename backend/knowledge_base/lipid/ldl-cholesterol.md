## What it measures
LDL stands for low-density lipoprotein. LDL particles carry cholesterol from the liver to cells around the body. LDL is often called **"bad" cholesterol** because, when there is too much of it, cholesterol can build up in the walls of the arteries. Over time this build-up, called plaque, can narrow the arteries and make heart attacks and strokes more likely. Many labs calculate LDL from the other lipid results, while some measure it directly.

## Why the test is done
LDL is usually the main number doctors use to judge cholesterol-related heart risk and to decide whether, and how, to manage cholesterol. It is part of a standard lipid profile and is checked during routine adult health checks, and more often for people with diabetes, heart disease or a family history of high cholesterol.

## Understanding the reference range
Many reports print bands such as: optimal, less than 100 mg/dL; near optimal, 100 to 129; borderline high, 130 to 159; high, 160 to 189; and very high, 190 or more. In mmol/L, 100 mg/dL is about 2.6. Your own target may be lower or higher depending on your heart risk, so the number on the report is a general guide. Your doctor can tell you what target applies to you.

## What high values can be associated with
LDL above the target can be associated with a diet high in saturated and trans fats, low physical activity, excess weight, smoking, genes (for example, familial hypercholesterolaemia), diabetes, an underactive thyroid, kidney conditions and some medicines. A high LDL is linked with a higher long-term risk of heart and blood vessel disease. Only your doctor can say what your result means for you.

## What low values can be associated with
A low LDL is generally considered good for the heart. Very low values are uncommon and can sometimes be associated with an overactive thyroid, liver conditions, poor nutrition or rare inherited conditions.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What LDL target is right for me?
- Given my LDL, what is my overall risk of heart disease?
- What could be contributing to my LDL level?
- When should I have my lipid profile repeated?
