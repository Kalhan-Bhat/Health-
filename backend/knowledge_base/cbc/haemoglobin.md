## What it measures
Haemoglobin (also spelled hemoglobin, and shortened to Hb or Hgb) is a protein inside red blood cells. It picks up oxygen in the lungs and carries it to every part of the body. It also helps carry carbon dioxide back to the lungs to be breathed out. Haemoglobin contains iron, which is why iron is important for making it. The test measures how much haemoglobin is in your blood, usually in grams per decilitre (g/dL).

## Why the test is done
Haemoglobin is part of a complete blood count. It is often checked during routine health checks, during pregnancy, before surgery, or when someone has symptoms such as tiredness, weakness, shortness of breath, dizziness or pale skin. It is also used to follow up known blood conditions.

## Understanding the reference range
Labs print their own range. For adults, ranges are often around 13 to 17 g/dL for men and 12 to 15.5 g/dL for women, but this varies between labs. Children, pregnant women and people living at high altitude can have different ranges. A result slightly outside the range may not be important. Doctors also look at hematocrit, red cell count and the red cell indices (MCV, MCH, MCHC) to understand a haemoglobin result.

## What high values can be associated with
Haemoglobin above the range can be associated with dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, some lung or heart conditions, and rarer conditions in which the body makes too many red cells. Many of these are not serious. Your doctor can tell what applies to you.

## What low values can be associated with
Haemoglobin below the range is often described as anaemia. It can have many causes, such as low iron, low vitamin B12 or folate, blood loss (for example heavy periods), pregnancy, long-term illness, kidney disease, or inherited conditions that affect haemoglobin. A low result is a starting point for your doctor to look for the reason, sometimes with extra tests such as iron studies.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- My haemoglobin is outside the range. What do you think could be causing it?
- Do I need any follow-up tests, such as iron studies or vitamin B12?
- Should this test be repeated, and when?
- Could any of my symptoms be related to this result?
