## What it measures
Platelets (also called thrombocytes) are tiny cell fragments in the blood. When you are cut or injured, platelets gather at the spot and stick together to help form a clot and stop the bleeding. The platelet count measures how many platelets are in a small amount of blood. Some reports also show the **MPV (mean platelet volume)**, which is the average size of the platelets.

## Why the test is done
The platelet count is part of a complete blood count. Doctors check it when someone bruises easily, bleeds for a long time, has tiny red spots on the skin, or before surgery. It is also used to follow conditions and treatments that affect the bone marrow, and to watch platelet levels during some infections, such as dengue fever.

## Understanding the reference range
A common adult range is about 1,50,000 to 4,10,000 platelets per microlitre (written 150,000 to 410,000, or 150 to 410 x10³/µL). Labs print their own range. Platelet counts can vary a little from day to day. Sometimes platelets clump together in the sample tube, which can make the count look falsely low. If so, the lab may suggest a repeat test.

## What high values can be associated with
A platelet count above the range (thrombocytosis) is often associated with the body reacting to something else. Examples include infection, inflammation, low iron, recent surgery or blood loss. Less often, it can be linked to conditions of the bone marrow. Your doctor can tell what applies to you.

## What low values can be associated with
A platelet count below the range (thrombocytopenia) can be associated with viral infections (including dengue), some medicines, immune conditions in which the body attacks its own platelets, liver conditions, pregnancy, low vitamin B12 or folate, and bone marrow conditions. Only your doctor can interpret a low result for you.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Is my platelet count high or low enough to need follow-up?
- Could platelet clumping in the sample have affected the result?
- Should I repeat the test, and should I watch for any signs such as unusual bruising?
