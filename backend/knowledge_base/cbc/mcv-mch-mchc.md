## What it measures
The red cell indices describe the size of your red blood cells and how much haemoglobin they hold. They help explain *why* haemoglobin might be low or high.
- **MCV (mean corpuscular volume)** is the average size of your red cells, measured in femtolitres (fL).
- **MCH (mean corpuscular haemoglobin)** is the average amount of haemoglobin in each red cell, measured in picograms (pg).
- **MCHC (mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration)** is how concentrated the haemoglobin is inside the cells, measured in g/dL.

## Why the test is done
These values come automatically with a complete blood count. Doctors use them mainly when haemoglobin is outside the range, because the size and colour of red cells point towards different groups of causes. Cells may be described as small (microcytic), normal-sized (normocytic) or large (macrocytic). Cells with less haemoglobin may be called hypochromic, meaning paler.

## Understanding the reference range
Typical adult ranges are around 80 to 100 fL for MCV, 27 to 32 pg for MCH, and 32 to 36 g/dL for MCHC, but each lab prints its own. Indices that are within range together with a normal haemoglobin are usually reassuring. Doctors read the indices as a group, together with RDW.

## What high values can be associated with
A high MCV (larger red cells) can be associated with low vitamin B12 or folate, regular heavy alcohol use, liver conditions, an underactive thyroid, some medicines, and other causes. A high MCHC is less common and can be linked to certain inherited red cell conditions or to sample issues.

## What low values can be associated with
A low MCV and MCH (smaller, paler cells) are often associated with low iron. They can also be associated with inherited conditions such as thalassaemia trait, and with long-term illness. Only your doctor can tell which applies to you, sometimes with extra tests.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- What do my red cell indices suggest about my haemoglobin result?
- Do I need iron studies or vitamin B12 and folate tests?
- Could an inherited condition such as thalassaemia trait explain my results?
