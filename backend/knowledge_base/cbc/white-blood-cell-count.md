## What it measures
White blood cells are part of the immune system, the body's defence against infections and other threats. The white blood cell (WBC) count measures how many of these cells are in a small amount of blood. Some labs in India call it the **total leucocyte count (TLC)**. It is usually reported as cells per microlitre (/µL or /cumm), or as a number times 10³/µL.

## Why the test is done
The WBC count is part of a complete blood count. Doctors use it to look for signs of infection or inflammation, to check the immune system, to follow conditions that affect the bone marrow, and to watch for side effects of some treatments, such as chemotherapy.

## Understanding the reference range
A common adult range is around 4,000 to 11,000 cells/µL, but each lab prints its own. The count naturally goes up and down a little during the day, after exercise, during stress and in pregnancy. A single result slightly outside the range is often not important, especially if you feel well. The differential (the mix of white cell types) gives more detail.

## What high values can be associated with
A WBC count above the range (sometimes called leucocytosis) is often associated with an infection or inflammation. It can also be associated with physical or emotional stress, smoking, some medicines such as steroids, allergies, and, less often, conditions of the bone marrow or blood. Your doctor can tell what applies to you.

## What low values can be associated with
A WBC count below the range (sometimes called leucopenia) can be associated with some viral infections, some medicines, autoimmune conditions, low vitamin levels, and conditions that affect the bone marrow. Some healthy people naturally have counts a little below the usual range. Only your doctor can interpret the result for you.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Is my white cell count high or low enough to need follow-up?
- Could a recent infection or a medicine explain this result?
- Which type of white cell is affected, according to my differential?
