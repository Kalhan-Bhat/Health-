## What it measures
Liver function tests (LFTs) are a group of blood tests that give information about the health of your liver. The liver processes nutrients, makes important proteins, stores energy, and helps remove waste and toxins. A typical panel includes:
- **ALT (also called SGPT)** and **AST (also called SGOT):** enzymes found inside liver cells. They leak into the blood when liver cells are irritated or damaged. AST is also found in muscles and the heart.
- **ALP (alkaline phosphatase):** an enzyme linked to the bile ducts and also to bones.
- **Bilirubin:** a yellow substance made when old red blood cells are broken down. The liver processes it so it can leave the body. Reports may show total, direct and indirect bilirubin.
- **Albumin and total protein:** proteins made by the liver.
- Sometimes **GGT**, another enzyme linked to the bile ducts.

## Why the test is done
LFTs are used in routine health checks, to look for causes of symptoms such as yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), dark urine, tummy pain or tiredness, to monitor known liver conditions, and to check for side effects of some medicines.

## Understanding the reference range
Each test has its own range, and ranges differ quite a lot between labs. As a rough guide, ALT and AST are often below about 40 U/L, and total bilirubin is often below about 1.2 mg/dL. Mildly raised liver enzymes are common and often settle without any treatment. Doctors look at the pattern of which tests are raised, and by how much, rather than at one number.

## What high values can be associated with
Raised ALT and AST can be associated with fatty liver, alcohol, viral hepatitis, some medicines and supplements, and muscle injury or intense exercise (for AST). Raised ALP can be associated with bile duct problems, bone growth in teenagers, pregnancy and bone conditions. Raised bilirubin can be associated with liver or bile duct problems, faster breakdown of red cells, and a common harmless inherited condition called Gilbert's syndrome. Only your doctor can tell what applies to you.

## What low values can be associated with
Low albumin or total protein can be associated with long-term liver conditions, kidney conditions that leak protein, poor nutrition and long-term illness. Low liver enzymes are usually not a concern.

## Questions you might ask your doctor
- Which of my liver tests are outside the range, and how much does that matter?
- Could any medicine, supplement, alcohol or exercise have affected these results?
- Do I need repeat tests or an ultrasound scan?
